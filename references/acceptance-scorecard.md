# Acceptance Scorecard

**Use this as a review aid, not a numeric gate or stopping condition.** Select checks that matter to this deliverable; a figure is not finished merely because it rendered. Use successive passes to discover issues the checklist cannot predict.

## Non-negotiable checks

Apply the checks that matter to the current task; “not applicable” is a valid result. A full paper-level audit is for a set-level task, not every isolated figure.

- **Scientific contract:** plotted claims, source, transformations, independent unit, comparison, and uncertainty are stated or traceable; resolve any ambiguity that could change the interpretation.
- **Truthfulness:** no invented marks, hidden tails, silent filtering, changed denominator, or unsupported schematic relationship.
- **Legibility:** essential text/marks are not clipped, obscured, or materially misleading at intended output size.
- **Comparability:** share scales when the comparison requires it; label intentional differences between scales or statistical objects.
- **Reproducibility:** preserve the source and renderer needed for the next person to understand or regenerate the figure, proportional to the deliverable.

## Review lenses

- **Hierarchy:** the primary answer is found first; context does not compete with evidence.
- **Rhythm:** corresponding panel letters, titles, rows, labels, and numeric columns align; spacing repeats intentionally.
- **Palette:** colours distinguish the intended concepts; pale marks have a darker companion; weak pairs gain shape/fill/line redundancy.
- **Typography:** type roles and weight are stable; the smallest text has a reason; numbers and units are easy to scan.
- **Economy:** every visible element earns space; decoration, duplicate legends, and long in-panel prose are removed.
- **Transfer:** the figure still reads when downscaled, printed, viewed in grayscale, or placed beside the paper's other figures.
- **Image context:** image-based panels identify scale, magnification, insets, boundaries, and colour/annotation keys where those details affect interpretation.

## Human review questions

Ask these after automated checks:

1. What should a reader say after five seconds? Is that the intended answer?
2. Does the composition make the evidence order obvious without the caption?
3. Does any colour or icon imply more causality, certainty, or mechanism than the data support?
4. Does the left/right or top/bottom pairing remain clear when labels have unequal length?
5. Would a different reasonable composition communicate the same evidence more honestly or economically?
6. Could a meaningful extra variable or domain context be integrated without making the main comparison harder to read? If not, would aligned panels show the relationship more clearly?

Use these questions to start a refinement pass, not to count a figure as finished. Where stakes or audience justify it, revisit the figure after fresh eyes or context; each pass should resolve a real uncertainty or improve a meaningful reading.

## Decision

- **Keep:** task-relevant non-negotiable checks pass and remaining concerns are understood and acceptable for the intended use.
- **Revise:** a non-negotiable check fails, or hierarchy, rhythm, palette, or typography needs another pass.
- **Rethink the direction:** the reference repeatedly distorts the data, forces unsupported symmetry, or requires exceptions that outweigh its reuse benefit.

Record the decision, failed checks, and human-review notes in the figure receipt. A numerical score may support comparison between drafts; it must not replace judgement.
