# Figure Craft

A personal visual craft system for scientific figures: aim for work that feels composed, calm, and quietly distinctive, with a sense of care in its typography, colour, spacing, and detail. Let the figure feel open and easy to stay with, while keeping its meaning precise.

**The bundled measurements are starting points, not fixed laws.** Let the question, evidence, medium, and audience decide the canvas, type, spacing, line work, and composition. Make each figure feel coherent on its own and in its wider set; bundled figures are references, not templates.

## Use it when

Use this skill for a scientific figure, graphical abstract, figure audit, or redesign. It works with a supplied figure, a rough sketch, a source table, or a blank page. Skip the full system for a tiny cosmetic edit that has no effect on meaning or layout.

## Operating shape

Begin by clarifying what the figure should say, what each panel contributes, and whether the evidence and any template fit. Then sketch, make, look again, and refine with as much care as the figure deserves. Keep the data honest, redesign supplied figures when needed, and consult journal rules only when a real submission requires them. Load references and checks only when they help.

## Core principles

- Begin with the scientific question; let visual encoding serve its meaning.
- Use Rose–Plum–Gold as the recognizable default: rose for the primary result, plum for structure, and gold for stimulus/input when present. Extend or replace it only when the science, audience, medium, accessibility, or explicit direction calls for it.
- Seek hierarchy, breathing room, and a quietly distinctive visual voice. A third variable, a richer composition, or an unconventional idea is welcome when it reveals a real relationship and remains easy to read.
- Preserve scientific truth while exploring form: do not invent data, hide observations, change estimands, or make schematic relationships look evidenced when they are not.
